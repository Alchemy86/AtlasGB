# The map you are standing on — Pokémon Red/Blue

[← Pokémon Red/Blue](../README.md) · [by address](by-address.md) · [by name](by-name.md) · [the structures](structures.md) · [AtlasGB](../../../README.md)

The map header is the record the engine reads a room out of: how big it is, which tileset,
where its blocks live, its warps, its signs, its objects, its connections to the maps beside
it. It is **saved state**, and that is the trap that has cost the most time to learn.

`LoadSAV` sets bit 7 of `wCurMapTileset` right after restoring the save, and `LoadMapHeader`
sees that bit, clears it, and **returns without loading anything**. So a continued game trusts
the header the save restored. Change `wCurMap` without changing the header and the engine reads
the new map's blocks through the old map's pointers — observed as a jump to `rst $38` or an
illegal opcode within a second. **You cannot teleport the player by editing the save.** What
does work is redirecting one of the room's own `wWarpEntries` and letting the player walk into
it, because the engine then loads the destination header itself.

That same latch is also the hook that makes [authoring a new area](https://github.com/Alchemy86/TerminalGB/blob/main/docs/gen1/authoring-an-area.md)
possible: it is the one moment the engine will build a room out of memory you supplied.

**Two units, and they are not the same.** `wCurMapHeight` and `wCurMapWidth` are in 32×32
*blocks*; `wYCoord` and `wXCoord` are in 16×16 *tiles*. Two tiles per block, in each axis.
Report the raw width to something that thinks in coordinates and it will conclude the player is
standing outside the map — which is exactly what an agent reading these bytes did before the
factor of two was applied.

Collision comes from the tileset, not from the map: `wTilesetCollisionPtr` is an
`$FF`-terminated list of the tile ids you may walk on, and everything not in it is solid.
