# Who you are — Pokémon Red/Blue

[← Pokémon Red/Blue](../README.md) · [by address](by-address.md) · [by name](by-name.md) · [the structures](structures.md) · [AtlasGB](../../../README.md)

Eight bytes decide almost everything about how the game treats you, and two of them are read
far more often than a player would guess.

**`wPlayerID` is not cosmetic.** It is copied into the OT field of every Pokémon you catch, and
`CheckForDisobedience` compares a Pokémon's OT ID against it to decide whether that Pokémon was
*traded* to you. A traded Pokémon obeys only up to a level your badges permit. So an injected
Pokémon carrying a made-up trainer ID is well-formed, passes every checksum, and then answers
*"won't obey!"* and wastes the turn. Anything that writes a party has to inherit the running
game's `wPlayerID` and `wPlayerName`, or it is correct for a state it invented and wrong for the
state it meant.

**Money is big-endian BCD**, three bytes, each nybble one decimal digit — so the cap is 999,999
and not 16,777,215. The harness checks every nybble, which is a surprisingly good test of the
address: a wrong one is very unlikely to hold six valid decimal digits.

Play time is here because the cartridge has no clock. Four counters — frames, seconds, minutes,
hours — carried by the frame interrupt, saved with everything else.
