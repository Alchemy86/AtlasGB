# Tables in the cartridge — Pokémon Red/Blue

[← Pokémon Red/Blue](../README.md) · [by address](by-address.md) · [by name](by-name.md) · [the structures](structures.md) · [AtlasGB](../../../README.md)

Work RAM is what the game is thinking; this is what it knows. A curated selection —
`pokeblue.map` names twenty-one thousand ROM symbols and all but a handful are code, so an
unfiltered sweep would bury the tables a reader actually wants.

Three of them are **verified against the cartridge by content**, which is the strongest grade
on any page here: the bytes at the address are read and compared against something
unmistakable. `MonsterNames` really does begin with RHYDON; `BaseStats` really does begin with
Bulbasaur's 45/49/49/45/65; `PokedexOrder` really does begin with its eight known dex numbers.
Two independent methods — the built disassembly and a byte search — agree on each address.

**The two species indexes are the thing to understand here.** `MonsterNames` is indexed by
*internal index*, `BaseStats` by *Pokédex number*, and `PokedexOrder` converts between them.
They are different orders, both 1-based, both plausible, and reading one table with the other's
index gives you a real Pokémon that is the wrong one. Mew's base stats live outside the main
table in Red and Blue and have to be special-cased.

Because these are the player's own cartridge's tables, the harness **locates them by byte
signature at run time** rather than by hard-coded offset, and cross-checks a later entry so a
one-record misalignment fails loudly. That is what keeps game data out of this repository: the
numbers are read from the cartridge in front of you, and only the *address* is written down.
