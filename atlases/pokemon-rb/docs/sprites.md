# The people on screen — Pokémon Red/Blue

[← Pokémon Red/Blue](../README.md) · [by address](by-address.md) · [by name](by-name.md) · [the structures](structures.md) · [AtlasGB](../../../README.md)

Sixteen slots, described twice. `wSpriteStateData1` holds screen position and animation;
`wSpriteStateData2` holds map position, identity and the movement script. **Slot 0 is the
player**; slots 1-15 are the map's objects, and this chapter's 859 entries are those two banks
of sixteen expanded field by field.

The one thing to know before writing anything here: **while a step is in progress the engine
owns every slot's screen pixels.** `wYCoord` and `wXCoord` stand still for the eight frames of
a walk while the camera scrolls, and the engine shifts every sprite two pixels a frame against
the player's motion so that they appear to stay where they are on the map. Recompute a sprite's
pixels from the player's coordinates during that window and the sprite rides the camera and
then snaps when the coordinates catch up. `wWalkCounter` is the gate: non-zero means stand off.

`wNumSprites` is more load-bearing than its name suggests. Two separate routines are gated on
it — the one that shifts sprites against the camera, and the one that decides what an A press
in front of an object resolves to — so raising it is what makes an extra object both *move
correctly* and *be talkable*, and doing one without the other is a bug.

`wShadowOAM` is the 160-byte shadow copied into the hardware by the DMA routine every frame.
**It is immediately followed by `wTileMap`** — `wShadowOAMEnd` and `wTileMap` are the same
address — so the eleventh drawn character writes over the screen. That is a hard cap, not a
guideline, and it is a cap the cartridge does not enforce: see below.

### Findings behind these bytes

| the finding | the bytes it is about |
|---|---|
| [the Oak's Lab soft-lock](discoveries.md#the-oaks-lab-soft-lock) | [`wShadowOAM`](#s-wShadowOAM), [`wShadowOAMEnd`](#s-wShadowOAMEnd), [`hOAMBufferOffset`](system.md#s-hOAMBufferOffset), [`wTileMap`](screen.md#s-wTileMap) |

**The overrun is a fault in the game, not a limit it enforces.** `PrepareOAMData` walks all
sixteen slots and writes four entries for each one that survives the visibility cull, tracking
the write position in an eight-bit offset that nothing bounds at forty entries; its clear loop
then stops only on an equality test against the low byte of `wShadowOAMEnd`. Start past that
and the test never fires again until the next page, so the loop stamps `$A0` — the value that
parks a sprite off the bottom of the screen — into `wTileMap` every fourth byte for 256 bytes.
A stamped tile is not walkable in any direction. Measured on a retail cartridge: 53 of 600
scripted attempts at one Oak's Lab route froze permanently on the same tile, with the picture
still being drawn and the play-time counter still ticking.
