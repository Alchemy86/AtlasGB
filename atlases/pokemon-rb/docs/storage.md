# Storage — the PC boxes — Pokémon Red/Blue

[← Pokémon Red/Blue](../README.md) · [by address](by-address.md) · [by name](by-name.md) · [the structures](structures.md) · [AtlasGB](../../../README.md)

Twelve boxes of twenty. **One of them is in work RAM and eleven are on the cartridge**, and
that split is the whole reason the storage system behaves the way it does.

The open box lives at `wBoxDataStart` in the same three-parallel-arrays shape as the party: a
count, a `$FF`-terminated species list, and twenty [`box_struct`](structures.md)s. A
`box_struct` is a `party_struct` with the level and the five computed stats removed — 33 bytes
against 44 — because a stored Pokémon's stats are *recalculated* from its experience and DVs
when it is withdrawn. That is also why depositing and withdrawing a Pokémon can change its
stats: the stored form has no memory of what they were.

The other eleven sit in cartridge RAM, boxes 1-6 in bank 2 and 7-12 in bank 3, `$462` bytes
apart. Three things about them are easy to get wrong:

* **Until the player has changed box once, banks 2 and 3 are uninitialised garbage.**
  `wCurrentBoxNum` bit 7 is the flag that says otherwise. Read a stored box before it is set
  and you decode noise.
* **The current box's cartridge-RAM slot is deliberately emptied.** Work RAM is authoritative
  for whichever box is open; the saved copy is written back when you switch.
* **Every stored box carries its own checksum**, six per bank. A box that fails it should be
  refused, not decoded — that is what the trainer plugin's collection reader does.

Cartridge RAM is unreachable over the bus almost all the time, because the game leaves it
disabled: a plain read of `$A000` returns open bus, not storage. The plugin contract's
`peek_sram_bank` exists for exactly this.

The Day Care's single lodger is here too. It is one `box_struct` on its own, belonging to no
box.
